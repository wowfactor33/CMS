The Periodic Table

In 1869, Dmitri Mendeleev published his periodic table of elements, arranging the known chemical elements by their atomic weights. This arrangement revealed patterns in the properties of the elements, which Mendeleev called the Periodic Law.

Mendeleev's table left gaps for undiscovered elements, and he predicted their properties based on the patterns he observed. For example, he predicted the existence of gallium, scandium, and germanium, describing their expected atomic weights, densities, and other characteristics.

Key Predictions:

  • Gallium (Ga): Predicted in 1871, discovered in 1875 by Paul Émile Lecoq de Boisbaudran.
  • Scandium (Sc): Predicted in 1871, discovered in 1879 by Lars Fredrik Nilson.
  • Germanium (Ge): Predicted in 1871, discovered in 1886 by Clemens Winkler.

Mendeleev's periodic table was revolutionary because it not only organized existing knowledge but also guided future discoveries. It has been refined over time but remains the cornerstone of modern chemistry.
